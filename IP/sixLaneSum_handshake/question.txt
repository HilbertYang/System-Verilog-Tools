Question A: 6-Lane 32-bit Adder (Single-Cycle with Handshake)

Design module sixLaneSum_handshake: sum six 32-bit inputs in one cycle using a
valid/ready handshake.

Ports:
  Inputs : clk, rst_n, in_valid, in_data0~in_data5 [31:0], out_ready
  Outputs: in_ready, out_valid, sum [31:0]

Behavior:
1. in_ready (combinational) = ~out_valid | out_hs, exactly as in one_stage_buffer.
2. On in_hs: sum ← sum of all six inputs, out_valid ← 1.
3. On out_hs with no new input: out_valid ← 0.
4. Support same-cycle replacement (in_hs and out_hs asserted together).

Critical timing risk:
  Directly chaining the additions of six 32-bit values gives a critical path of
  approximately five adder delays, making high-frequency timing difficult.

Question B: 6-Lane 32-bit Adder (2-Stage Pipeline)

Starting from Question A, design module sixLaneSum_pipe2 with the addition split
into two pipeline stages:

Stage 1: Calculate three partial sums in parallel
  ps01 = in_data0 + in_data1
  ps23 = in_data2 + in_data3
  ps45 = in_data4 + in_data5

Stage 2: Add the three partial sums
  sum = ps01 + ps23 + ps45

Decouple the stages with an independent valid/ready handshake (s1_valid / s1_ready).

Ports (same as Question A):
  Inputs : clk, rst_n, in_valid, in_data0~in_data5 [31:0], out_ready
  Outputs: in_ready, out_valid, sum [31:0]

Handshake relationships:
  in_ready = ~s1_valid | s1_hs    (stage 1 buffer empty or consumed in the same cycle)
  s1_ready = ~out_valid | out_hs  (stage 2 buffer empty or consumed in the same cycle)

Interview follow-ups:
1. Why does a two-stage pipeline improve timing?
   → Stage 1 critical path: one adder delay. Stage 2: two adder delays
     (roughly halving the depth).
2. Why is a handshake needed between Stage 1 and Stage 2?
   → Under downstream backpressure, Stage 2 has valid output that cannot be
     consumed. Stage 1 must not overwrite Stage 2's data.
3. Can the addition be split into three stages?
   → Yes. Keep three parallel pair sums in Stage 1, add two partial sums in
     Stage 2, and perform the final addition in Stage 3, reducing the critical
     path to one adder delay.
